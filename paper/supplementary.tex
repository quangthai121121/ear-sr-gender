\noindent This document supplements \emph{``Does Super-Resolution Improve Ear-Based Gender Classification? Controlled Evidence for Deployment Decisions''} (DASA'26). It contains three tables: Table~S1 extends Table~IV of the main paper to all input variants, Table~S2 reports the per-class metrics behind the recall and balanced-accuracy statements in Section~IV-B, and Table~S3 reports the label-free adaptation results behind Section~IV-F.

All rows use the same-checkpoint protocol: each backbone is trained once on Original images of the respective protocol under the locked configuration and tested on all four inputs. Protocol~A holds out 10\% of its training half for early stopping; its test half is the same as in the legacy split.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Summary.} The Protocol~A$\to$B gap is positive for all 24 combinations (range 0.74--4.62 pp, mean 2.45 pp). The mean gap is similar across input variants (Original 2.42, Bicubic 2.29, Real-ESRGAN 2.54, SwinIR 2.56 pp), but it varies more across backbones for the SR inputs (0.74--4.62 pp) than for Original and Bicubic (1.78--3.01 pp), because the same-checkpoint SR penalty differs between protocols for some backbones. Early-stopping Protocol~A on its test half instead of the held-out split would have raised its Original accuracy by a further 0.5 pp on average. The inflation from image-level splitting is therefore not specific to one input variant. Absolute accuracies reported under an image-level split, as in the original EarVN1.0 gender study, should not be compared directly with subject-disjoint numbers, with or without super-resolution.

\section*{S3. Label-free adaptation of the same checkpoint}
\noindent Table~S3 lists the results of adapting each Original-trained Protocol~B checkpoint to SR inputs without labels or retraining, as described in Section~III-C of the main paper. The adaptation set is each fold's validation images, used without labels. \emph{Threshold} shifts the female-minus-male logit margin so that the fraction of SR inputs predicted female equals the fraction the same checkpoint predicts on the Original versions of those images. \emph{AdaBN} re-estimates the BatchNorm statistics on the SR images and applies only to the four backbones with BatchNorm, so VGG19 and Swin-T have no AdaBN rows. $\Delta$Acc is the accuracy change versus the same checkpoint on Original inputs; all values are means over the 5 folds.

Threshold recalibration improves on no adaptation in 53 of 60 backbone-fold-SR cases and AdaBN in 38 of 40. The remaining loss stays significant: fold-level 95\% confidence intervals of $\Delta$Acc are $[-1.92, -0.95]$ (Real-ESRGAN) and $[-1.67, -0.89]$ (SwinIR) for threshold recalibration, and $[-1.61, -0.76]$ and $[-0.87, -0.65]$ for AdaBN. Averaged over both SR methods and the six backbones, male recall rises from 87.93\% without adaptation to 93.31\% with threshold recalibration (Original: 94.59\%). The summary rows give the values quoted in Section~IV-F.
